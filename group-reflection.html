The Reflection

Five Stages of Group Development

Psychologist Bruce Tuckman's model explains how groups can develop from their first meeting into teams that work together effectively. Each stage presents different experiences and opportunities for growth.

Stage 01 · Getting Started

Forming

The forming stage is when group members first come together, get to know one another and begin to understand the task. Members may be polite and enthusiastic, but they may also be uncertain about their responsibilities and how the group will operate.

At this stage, I recognise the importance of listening, introducing ideas respectfully and understanding what the assignment requires. Getting to know the people I am working with can help build trust and make communication easier.

My reflection: I should consider how our group was formed, whether we knew each other beforehand and how we initially shared ideas and responsibilities.
Stage 02 · Facing Differences

Storming

During storming, differences in opinions, working styles, availability or expectations may become more noticeable. Members might disagree about ideas or how to divide the work. These challenges do not automatically mean that a group is failing.

I understand that disagreements should be handled with patience and respect. Instead of taking different opinions personally, group members can explain their reasoning, listen to one another and focus on finding a solution that supports the shared objective.

My reflection: I should describe a real challenge we experienced, how it affected our work and what we actually did to address it.
Stage 03 · Finding Our Rhythm

Norming

In the norming stage, a group begins to establish shared expectations, routines and a stronger sense of responsibility. Members become more familiar with their roles and learn how to coordinate their contributions.

Clear communication, agreed deadlines and fair task allocation can help create this structure. I recognise that teamwork improves when everyone understands what is expected of them and feels that their contribution is valued.

My reflection: I should identify the actual routines or agreements our group developed, such as meeting arrangements, communication methods or task deadlines.
Stage 04 · Working Effectively

Performing

During performing, the group is able to focus more effectively on completing its work. Members cooperate, solve problems and take responsibility for their assigned tasks. Communication and trust help the group work towards its goal.

This stage reminds me that completing my own task is only one part of contributing to a team. I should also consider how my work fits into the final outcome, support other members where appropriate and communicate early if I experience difficulties.

My reflection: I should explain what I personally contributed, how the group combined its work and what evidence shows that we made progress.
Stage 05 · Looking Back

Adjourning

Adjourning happens when the group's task comes to an end. Members can reflect on what they achieved, recognise each other's contributions and consider what they would do differently next time.

I see this stage as an opportunity to evaluate both the final outcome and the process we followed. Reflecting on our strengths and difficulties can help me become a more responsible and effective team member in future projects.

My reflection: I should evaluate our actual outcome, what I learned from the experience and what I would improve in another group assignment.
Lessons I Can Carry Forward

What Makes a Team Work?

The group-development model highlights practical habits that can make collaboration more productive and enjoyable.

✦

Communication

Share ideas clearly, listen actively and ask questions when expectations or instructions are unclear.

◇

Accountability

Complete assigned tasks on time, keep other members informed and take responsibility for my contribution.

♡

Respect

Recognise that people have different perspectives and working styles, and treat those differences constructively.

Personal Development

How I Want to Grow as a Team Member

As a first-year student, I am still developing my approach to collaborative learning. Group assignments give me opportunities to practise skills that will be useful throughout my studies and in my future career in data analytics.

Data analytics often involves interpreting information, explaining findings and working with people who have different responsibilities and perspectives. Being able to cooperate, communicate clearly and meet deadlines will therefore be important to my professional growth.

  • Contribute ideas confidently while remaining open to feedback.
  • Agree on roles, responsibilities and deadlines early.
  • Address misunderstandings respectfully instead of ignoring them.
  • Check in with group members and communicate progress regularly.
  • Reflect on feedback after each project and apply it next time.

Final Thoughts

Every Group Is a Learning Experience.

My main takeaway is that effective teamwork develops through communication, trust, shared responsibility and reflection. Understanding Tuckman's stages gives me a framework for recognising group challenges and thinking about how I can contribute more positively to future collaborations.
