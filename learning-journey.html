NOTES FROM MY FIRST YEAR

The learning curve.

A reflection on starting something new, adjusting to a different way of learning and discovering that understanding takes time.

STUDENT REFLECTION 2026 APPROX. 450 WORDS

A PERSONAL ACCOUNT

I came here to learn. I didn't realise how much I would have to unlearn.

My experience as a first-year Business Intelligence and Data Analytics student.

Starting university felt like stepping into a new environment with a familiar goal: to learn. However, I quickly realised that learning at this level requires more than simply attending classes and remembering information.

Adjusting to a different pace

One of the biggest adjustments has been understanding that university learning requires a greater level of independence. There is more responsibility placed on me to manage my time, keep up with my work and take initiative when something is unclear. Nobody can do the learning for me, and that is something I am still getting used to.

Studying Business Intelligence and Data Analytics has also introduced me to ways of thinking that I am still developing. Some topics make sense immediately, while others require me to slow down, revisit the material and approach it differently. I have had to accept that not understanding something the first time does not mean I am incapable of learning it.

Discovering how I learn

I am beginning to understand that different subjects require different approaches. Reading notes alone is not always enough. Sometimes I need to work through examples, practise a concept or explain it in my own words before it actually makes sense.

I have also learnt that asking questions is important. It can be tempting to remain quiet when something is confusing, but leaving a gap in understanding usually makes the next topic harder. I am learning to be more comfortable with not knowing something and doing what I can to understand it.

The part that isn't always easy

Balancing different responsibilities and keeping consistent with academic work can be challenging. There are moments when the workload feels like a lot, especially when several things require attention at the same time. I am still working on managing my time better and avoiding the habit of waiting until something becomes urgent before giving it attention.

I would not say that I have everything figured out. In fact, recognising what I still need to improve has been one of the more useful parts of this experience. Growth is not always obvious, and sometimes it looks like simply doing better than I did the previous time.

Looking ahead

As I continue with my studies, I want to become more confident in applying what I learn rather than only focusing on passing assessments. I want to strengthen my technical understanding, become a better problem solver and develop a clearer picture of how data analytics works outside the classroom.

My long-term goal is to become a data analyst and eventually establish my own analytics firm. I know that this will require patience, discipline and a willingness to keep learning. For now, I am focusing on building a foundation, one concept and one experience at a time.

If there is one thing my first year has reminded me of, it is that I do not need to have the entire journey figured out to take the next step. I just need to remain curious, stay willing to learn and keep showing up.

END OF REFLECTION

— Tinashe Motshubi
