01 / COLLABORATION & TEAMWORK

NO ONE BUILDS EVERYTHING ALONE

Better together.

Working with other people means bringing different personalities, ideas and approaches into one space. It is not always straightforward, but there is something to learn from the process.

02

UNDERSTANDING THE PROCESS

Groups don't become teams overnight.

A group is more than a collection of people assigned the same task.

People have different ways of communicating, organising their work and approaching problems. Getting everyone to work towards the same goal takes time, understanding and a bit of patience.

Tuckman's model explains how groups can develop through five stages. These stages provide a useful way of understanding the different experiences that can happen during group work.

THE MODEL

Bruce Tuckman's Five Stages of Group Development

Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning

The stages are a framework for understanding group development. Not every group moves through them in exactly the same way.

01

THE BEGINNING

STAGE ONE

Forming

Getting to know the people behind the task.

This is the stage where a group comes together. Members are introduced, the task is understood and everyone begins figuring out what is expected. At this point, people may still be reserved because they are unfamiliar with one another.

MY REFLECTION

I think the beginning of group work is important because it sets the tone for everything that follows. People need an opportunity to understand the task, share initial ideas and become comfortable enough to communicate. I would rather have a group that takes time to establish a direction than one that rushes into the work without knowing what it is trying to achieve.

02

THE CHALLENGE

STAGE TWO

Storming

Different opinions start finding their voices.

During this stage, differences may become more noticeable. Members might disagree about ideas, responsibilities, deadlines or how the task should be completed. This can create tension, but it can also help a group recognise issues that need to be addressed.

MY REFLECTION

I believe disagreement is not automatically a bad thing. People do not have to think alike to work together. What matters is how those differences are handled. Listening, explaining your reasoning and being willing to reconsider an idea can make a big difference. The goal should be finding a workable solution, not proving who is right.

03

FINDING BALANCE

STAGE THREE

Norming

Learning how to work with one another.

The group begins developing a shared understanding of how it should operate. Responsibilities become clearer, communication improves and members begin recognising the value each person brings.

MY REFLECTION

This stage reminds me that teamwork needs some structure. It helps when everyone understands their responsibilities and knows how their contribution fits into the bigger picture. Clear communication also makes it easier to identify problems before they become bigger than they need to be.

04

GETTING THINGS DONE

STAGE FOUR

Performing

When collaboration starts to work.

At this stage, the group is able to work more effectively towards its objectives. Members understand their roles, communicate more confidently and focus on completing the task. There is a stronger sense of shared responsibility.

MY REFLECTION

To me, performing is where the effort put into the earlier stages becomes visible. A group should not depend on one person doing everything. Everyone needs to take ownership of their part while remaining willing to help when necessary. I think the best results come when individual contributions support one shared outcome.

05

THE CONCLUSION

STAGE FIVE

Adjourning

Finishing the task and taking the lessons forward.

This is the stage where the group completes its work and members begin moving on to other responsibilities. It is an opportunity to reflect on what was achieved, recognise individual contributions and consider what could be improved next time.

MY REFLECTION

I think finishing a group task should involve more than simply submitting the work. It is worth looking back at how the group functioned, what worked well and what could have been handled differently. Every group experience can teach something useful about communication, responsibility and working with people.

03

WHAT THE MODEL HAS TAUGHT ME

Teamwork is a skill in itself.

01

Communication

People cannot work towards the same goal if they do not understand one another. Speaking clearly and listening properly are equally important.

02

Accountability

Every member has a responsibility to contribute. A team's progress should not rest entirely on one person's effort.

03

Respect

Different opinions and working styles should be approached with patience rather than treated as obstacles.

04

Adaptability

Sometimes the original plan needs adjusting. Being flexible helps a group respond to challenges without losing sight of its objective.

ONE LAST THOUGHT

Good teamwork isn't about thinking alike.

It is about learning how to think, communicate and work alongside people who may see things differently. That is a lesson worth carrying beyond the classroom.
